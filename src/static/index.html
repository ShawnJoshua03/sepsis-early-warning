Sepsis early warning

A gradient-boosting model scores ICU patients every hour from routine vitals and labs and tries to flag sepsis up to 12 hours before clinical onset. Below, real held-out patients from the PhysioNet 2019 Challenge are replayed hour by hour, including the cases where it fails.

How to read the chart

The teal line is the model’s risk score each hour. When it crosses the dashed red threshold an alert fires. The shaded band is the window the Challenge metric rewards: 12 hours before clinical onset to 3 hours after. An alert inside it counts as caught; an alert before it is too early to be useful.

How it is scored

The Challenge utility metric gives the most credit for alerts 6 to 12 hours before onset, penalizes late or missed cases hard, and charges 0.05 for every false-alarm hour. Features at hour t only use data up to t. Train and test are split by patient.

Where it falls short

  • High alarm burden: 59% of non-septic test patients alarm at least once. A real deployment would need alert suppression or a stricter threshold.
  • Tested on one hospital system only; no external validation yet.
  • Probabilities are not calibrated. See the README limitations.
